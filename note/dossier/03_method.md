## 3. The fixes — six flaws, and the frozen network {#method}

This section defends the experimental design. It makes three separable promises: that 6 data-pipeline flaws (of 9 diagnosed) were fixed, that the *fixes* — not some architecture change — are what moved the score, and that the network was frozen so tightly across the comparison that it cannot be the confound. Everything below is written so an interviewer can check each promise against a committed file.

!!! intuition "The one-liner"
    Change the pipeline, freeze the network. The six fixed data-pipeline flaws are the independent variable; the 1.98M-parameter U-Net is the *controlled* variable, held byte-identical so the gain belongs to the fixes and nothing else.

---

### "Fixed 6 data-pipeline flaws" — nine diagnosed

**Claim.** *"Fixed 6 data-pipeline flaws"* — the CV cites the six data-pipeline flaws that drove the accuracy gain. The full engineering audit found **nine** errors (labelled **D1–D9**); these six are the ones reproduced in the flawed pipeline and fixed in the corrected one, so their effect can be isolated (the other three are handled on both arms — see the table).

**What it means.** The reference code was a Colab export (`unet_cc.py`, header *"Copy of Untitled7.ipynb … Automatically generated by Colab"*) with no author, README, or LICENSE, carrying a BraTS-2020-era label remap onto 2024 data. Rather than "improve" it globally, each defect was isolated so its effect could be attributed. Six of the nine are *reproduced faithfully* in Track A and *only* fixed in Track B — those are the independent variable of the A/B. The other three are handled specially and are noted as such below, because pretending they were part of the A/B would overstate the result.

| # | Defect (reference behaviour) | Fix (Track B) | Why it matters | A/B status |
|---|------------------------------|---------------|----------------|------------|
| **D1** | Bilinear `cv2.resize` (`INTER_LINEAR`) on the **label** volume → fractional labels invent/destroy voxels | Nearest-neighbour resampling for labels | Interpolating a categorical mask fabricates classes; it inflates NETC to **105/105** and ET to **100/105** GT-present cases vs the honest **48** and **85** | **Reproduced in A** |
| **D2** | Per-class Dice shape/index bug → original logs untrustworthy | Correct MONAI `DiceMetric` | If you can't measure per-class Dice correctly you can't compare tracks at all | **Fixed on BOTH** (you must measure right to compare) |
| **D3** | Class imbalance unhandled; plain cross-entropy | `DiceCELoss` + class-balanced patch sampling | CE is dominated by the ~99% background voxels, so the net minimises loss by predicting background → near-zero minority Dice | **Reproduced in A** |
| **D4** | Checkpoint selected on **validation voxel accuracy** | Select on **mean foreground Dice** | Accuracy of **0.991** is reachable by predicting all-background; it picks a degenerate checkpoint (epochs 18–22) whose mean-fg Dice is only 0.320 | **Reproduced in A** |
| **D5** | Global-max normalisation `X / max(X)` | Per-modality z-score over brain (nonzero) voxels | One bright voxel rescales the whole volume and flattens cross-modality contrast the network needs | **Reproduced in A** |
| **D6** | Non-uniform slice stride `int(j·2.5)`, 48 slices | Foreground crop + 96³ class-balanced patches | Irregular anisotropic sampling drops thin/small lesions before the network ever sees them | **Reproduced in A** |
| **D7** | Dead label remap merging **RC→ET** — a 4-class contract on 5-class data | Assert 5-class `{0,1,2,3,4}` at load | Silently deletes the resection-cavity class; a 2020-era assumption smuggled onto 2024 post-treatment data | **Enforced on BOTH** (5-class asserted at load) |
| **D8** | No normalisation layers in the network | *(left as-is)* | A genuine handicap — but shared, so it is **held constant on BOTH** tracks and is explicitly *not* part of the A/B | **Held constant** |
| **D9** | Uncached, I/O-bound per-epoch loader | MONAI `PersistentDataset` cache | Pure engineering speed: ~23 s → ~8 s/epoch (~2.8×); no accuracy effect | **Reproduced in A** |

So Track A reproduces **D1, D3, D4, D5, D6, D9**; **D2** is fixed on both (you cannot run a fair comparison with a broken metric); **D7** is *enforced* on both (the 5-class contract is a correctness guardrail, not a tuning knob); **D8** is *held constant* on both (an equal handicap — see the parameter discussion below). Naming this split honestly is the point: the headline "+0.321 Dice" is driven by the six A-reproduced defects, and I do not claim credit for the three that are shared or enforced.

**Evidence.** The defect ladder shows how the six A-reproduced fixes stack from the collapsed Track A baseline up to Track B:

![Defect ladder — how the D1/D3/D4/D5/D6/D9 fixes stack from Track A to Track B](figures/diagrams/s20_defect_ladder.png)

D1 is the most vivid single defect, because it corrupts the *labels* before any learning happens. Bilinear-resized masks report NETC in **105/105** GT-present TEST cases and ET in **100/105**; the honest nearest-neighbour labels show only **48** and **85** (SNFH 105/105 and RC 87/87 agree on both — the prevalent classes are robust, the sparse ones are fabricated):

![D1 count inflation — bilinear label resampling invents NETC and ET presence](reports/figures/report/count_inflation.png)

**Verify.** The A-vs-B defect handling is diffable directly: `configs/track_a.yaml` (`label_interpolation: bilinear`, `normalization: global_max`, `slice_sampling: "…int(j*2.5)"`, `loss: cross_entropy`, `metric_selection: val_accuracy`, `cache: none`) against `configs/track_b.yaml` (the matching fixes). The D1 count-inflation numbers are recomputed in `scripts/compute_dossier_facts.py`.

!!! gotcha "Watch out"
    D2, D7, and D8 are *not* A/B knobs. If someone counts "nine fixes → +0.321 Dice", correct them: the delta comes from the six A-reproduced defects. D2 and D7 are correctness prerequisites shared by both tracks, and D8 is a deliberate shared handicap.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Name three defects and why each matters."**
    D1: bilinear `cv2.resize` on a categorical label volume interpolates *class indices*, fabricating voxels — it inflates NETC presence from an honest 48 to 105 of 105 TEST cases. D4: selecting the checkpoint on voxel accuracy is degenerate because 0.991 accuracy is achievable by predicting only background, so it locks in a model with 0.320 mean-fg Dice. D3: with plain cross-entropy the ~99% background dominates the loss, so the network minimises it by ignoring the tumour — fixed with `DiceCELoss` plus class-balanced patch sampling. All three are reproduced in Track A and fixed in Track B.

### "1.98M-param"

**Claim.** *"1.98M-param"* — the network has exactly **1,983,069 trainable parameters**.

**What it means.** A 3D convolutional layer with $c_\text{in}$ input channels, $c_\text{out}$ output channels and a $k \times k \times k$ kernel holds $c_\text{in}\cdot c_\text{out}\cdot k^3$ weights plus $c_\text{out}$ bias terms:

$$ N_\text{layer} = c_\text{in}\, c_\text{out}\, k^3 + c_\text{out}. $$

Summing this over every convolution in the encoder, decoder, and the final 1×1×1 output head — with $k=3$ everywhere except the 5-channel head — gives the total. The count is grouped by the feature width at which the parameters live; the deepest two stages (widths 64 and 256) dominate because parameter count scales with the *product* of adjacent widths.

| Feature width (channels) | Trainable params |
|---|---|
| 16 | 1,328 |
| 32 | 18,208 |
| 64 | 746,624 |
| 128 | 331,904 |
| 256 | 884,992 |
| **Total (`count_params`)** | **1,983,069** |

![Parameter budget by feature width — the 64- and 256-channel stages dominate](reports/figures/dossier/param_breakdown.png)

**Why so small.** 1.98M parameters is tiny for a 3D segmentation network (modern nnU-Net-class models run tens of millions). That is deliberate. The original network compounded the handicap with **D8** — no normalisation layers — and rather than "fix" the capacity to flatter the result, the small, norm-free network is **carried equally by both tracks**. An equal handicap on both sides *cancels* in the A→B difference: it can lower both absolute numbers but it cannot manufacture a delta, because the same 1,983,069 parameters compute both tracks. If anything it makes the +0.321 gain *conservative* — a bigger network would likely widen it.

**Verify.** `src/brats/model.py::count_params` returns 1,983,069 for both tracks (run its `__main__` block, which prints the count for track `a` and track `b`); the width breakdown is regenerated into `reports/figures/dossier/param_breakdown.png`.

!!! gotcha "Watch out"
    The per-width buckets are an *attribution by encoder stage*; they sum to ~1.983M and the exact, authoritative total is whatever `count_params` returns — **1,983,069**. Always quote the `count_params` number, not a hand-summed one.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Why such a tiny model — isn't that the real problem?"**
    No — and this is the crucial point. The 1.98M-parameter, normalisation-free network is a genuine handicap, but it is applied *equally* to Track A and Track B: both call the same factory with the same config, so both compute with the identical 1,983,069 parameters. An equal handicap subtracts out of the A→B difference; it can depress both absolute Dice scores but cannot create the +0.321 gap. The small model makes the result *conservative*, not inflated.

### "MONAI 3D U-Net"

**Claim.** *"MONAI 3D U-Net"* — the architecture is MONAI's `UNet`, configured for volumetric input.

**What it means.** MONAI (Medical Open Network for AI) is a PyTorch-based framework for medical imaging that ships vetted network, transform, and metric implementations; using its `UNet` means the architecture is a standard, auditable building block rather than bespoke code. The U-Net itself — an encoder that halves spatial resolution while doubling feature width, a symmetric decoder, and skip connections that reunite fine detail with deep context — is derived from first principles in the companion explainer (see **§11**). Here the exact instantiation is:

```
spatial_dims = 3, in_channels = 3, out_channels = 5,
channels = (16, 32, 64, 128, 256), strides = (2, 2, 2, 2),
num_res_units = 0, dropout = 0.1
```

`in_channels = 3` are the three retained modalities (t2f, t1c, t2w — native t1n dropped, matching the reference); `out_channels = 5` are background plus the four tumour classes (NETC, SNFH, ET, RC); `num_res_units = 0` makes it a plain (non-residual) U-Net.

**Verify.** This block lives once, in `configs/base.yaml` under `model:`, and is consumed by `src/brats/model.py::build_model`.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Why MONAI, and why a plain U-Net with no residual units?"**
    MONAI gives standard, tested implementations of the network, transforms, and the `DiceMetric` — so the comparison rests on vetted components, not code I might have gotten subtly wrong. `num_res_units = 0` keeps the network faithful to the small, plain reference architecture; adding residual units would have been an architecture change, and the whole design holds the architecture fixed so the delta belongs to the pipeline.

### "held byte-identical"

**Claim.** *"held byte-identical across the two tracks"* — the architecture is not "similar" between tracks; it is the *same object*, built from the same config block by the same factory.

**What it means.** Both tracks call `build_model(cfg)` in `src/brats/model.py`, and both `cfg['model']` sections resolve to the single `model:` block in `configs/base.yaml` via `defaults: base.yaml`. Neither `track_a.yaml` nor `track_b.yaml` overrides a single architecture key. Everything that differs between the tracks lives in the *pipeline* — loss, resampling, normalisation, sampling, selection, caching — never in the network. Laid out as a control table:

| Row | Track A | Track B | Same? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Architecture config** | UNet 16-32-64-128-256, strides 2/2/2/2, `num_res_units=0`, dropout 0.1 | UNet 16-32-64-128-256, strides 2/2/2/2, `num_res_units=0`, dropout 0.1 | **IDENTICAL** |
| Loss | plain `cross_entropy` (D3) | `DiceCELoss(include_background=False)` | differs |
| Label resampling | `bilinear` (D1) | `nearest` | differs |
| Normalization | `global_max`, `X/max(X)` (D5) | z-score per-modality, nonzero (D5 fix) | differs |
| Sampling | slice stride `int(j·2.5)`, 128×128×48 whole volume (D6) | 96³ class-balanced patches (D3/D6 fix) | differs |
| Checkpoint metric | `val_accuracy` (D4) | `mean_fg_dice` (D4 fix) | differs |
| Caching | `none` (D9) | `PersistentDataset` (D9 fix) | differs |

The top row is byte-identical; every other row is a pipeline knob. Because the architecture keys are defined *once* and inherited, there is literally no place for the two networks to diverge — the 1,983,069-parameter count printed for track `a` equals the count printed for track `b`.

**Verify.** Diff `configs/track_a.yaml` against `configs/track_b.yaml`: neither file contains a `model:` key, so both inherit `base.yaml` untouched. Then run `src/brats/model.py`'s `__main__`, which builds and counts *both* tracks and prints the identical 1,983,069.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Prove the architecture was byte-identical."**
    The `model:` block exists in exactly one file, `configs/base.yaml`. Both `track_a.yaml` and `track_b.yaml` declare `defaults: base.yaml` and override *only* pipeline keys (loss, interpolation, normalization, sampling, metric, cache) — grep them and you'll find no `model:` key in either. Both tracks pass their merged config to the same `build_model` factory, and `count_params` returns 1,983,069 for both. Same file, same factory, same count: identical by construction, not by inspection.

### "the two tracks"

**Claim.** *"the two tracks"* — the entire study is a single controlled A/B.

**What it means.** **Track A** is the *faithful* reproduction: it deliberately re-commits defects D1, D3, D4, D5, D6, D9 and is designed to fail in a documented way — validation voxel accuracy **0.991** masking a mean-fg Dice of just **0.320** and NETC Dice of **0.009**, with the best (accuracy-selected) checkpoint at epochs 18–22. **Track B** applies all the fixes: val accuracy 0.995, mean-fg Dice 0.682, checkpoints at epochs 64–72. Both share the enforced 5-class contract (D7), the correct metric (D2), and the identical handicapped network (D8, and the 1.98M parameters). The gap between them — held-out TEST mean-fg Dice **0.349 → 0.670, Δ = +0.321** — is thus attributable to the pipeline fixes and to nothing about the model, because the model was the controlled variable throughout.

!!! gotcha "Watch out"
    These are **voxel-wise** Dice scores, not the BraTS lesion-wise score — internally consistent for this A/B but **not comparable to challenge leaderboards**. And the ±SD is over 3 *seeds* on one fixed 105-case test split: it measures reproducibility, not population generalization.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"In one breath, what makes this a clean experiment?"**
    One independent variable — the six A-reproduced pipeline defects — moved between Track A and Track B, while the network was held byte-identical (same `base.yaml` `model:` block, same `build_model`, same 1,983,069 parameters) and the metric and 5-class contract were fixed on both sides. So the +0.321 Dice delta is caused by the fixes, not by anything I changed about the model — because I changed nothing about the model.
