## 2. Bullet 2 — "Curated 700 BraTS-2024 post-treatment glioma MRIs (3 modalities) into a stratified 490/105/105 split" {#data}

This bullet is the *data-provenance* claim. It has to survive the two questions any careful reviewer asks about a medical-imaging dataset: **where did it come from, and what did you actually do to it?** The honest answer is that I did no manual annotation whatsoever — "curated" here means quality-control, contract-checking, and a reproducible split, all recomputable from committed artifacts. Everything below is designed to make that boundary explicit rather than let the word "curated" over-claim.

!!! intuition "The one-liner"
    I did not label a single voxel. I *verified* 700 already-labelled volumes against a strict integer label contract, confirmed they were the post-treatment 2024 vintage, and cut them into a frozen, stratified 490/105/105 split at seed 42 — a data-engineering claim, not an annotation claim.

### "Curated 700"

**Claim.** *"Curated 700 … MRIs."* The dataset is **700 cases** pulled from a Kaggle mirror (`i212385nomanarif/2024-brats-glioma`) of the BraTS-2024 post-treatment glioma collection (BraTS-GLI).

**What it means.** "Curated" is doing a specific, limited job here and it is essential not to inflate it. It does **not** mean I hand-annotated the tumours — expert neuroradiologist segmentation of a glioma takes hours per case and is exactly the labour the BraTS challenge organisers already funded. What I did is *curation in the data-engineering sense*:

1. **Verified the label contract.** Every segmentation mask was checked to be strictly integer-valued with values a subset of $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ — i.e. no stray floats, no out-of-range indices, no silent class introduced by an upstream resample. This is not a formality: defect **D1** (§20) is precisely a case where a bilinear resize of a *label* volume invents fractional, then rounded, phantom classes. Contract-checking at load time is what catches that.
2. **Confirmed the vintage** (that these really are post-treatment 2024 scans, not a pre-operative set mislabelled — see the next subsection).
3. **Gzip-on-arrival.** The mirror ships raw `.nii`; each volume was gzip-compressed on ingest to keep the on-disk footprint manageable (the recorded working subset is ~4.9 GB on disk for its slice).

**Evidence & Verify.** The dataset identity, case count, and the resampled geometry caveat are recorded in the committed provenance stamp `reports/data_provenance.json` (`"dataset": "kaggle:i212385nomanarif/2024-brats-glioma"`, `modalities_kept: [t2f, t1c, t2w]`, a content SHA-256 prefix, and an explicit geometry note). The label-contract audit — the per-label present-counts across all 700 cases — is regenerated by `scripts/compute_dossier_facts.py` and frozen in `reports/dossier_facts.json` under `stratification.label_present`.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Does 'curated' mean you annotated the scans?"**
    No — and I'm careful to say so. The expert voxel-level segmentations were produced by the BraTS-2024 organisers and their neuroradiology annotators; I never drew a mask. My curation was quality control: I verified every mask is integer-valued with labels $\subseteq\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, confirmed the post-treatment vintage, gzip-compressed on ingest, and produced a reproducible split. If I claimed to have annotated 700 gliomas, that would be false; the CV verb is "curated" precisely to stay honest about that boundary.

### "BraTS-2024"

**Claim.** *"BraTS-2024."* The source is the **2024 edition** of the Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) challenge.

**What it means.** BraTS is the long-running international benchmark for brain-tumour segmentation: the organisers release multi-parametric MRI volumes with expert consensus labels and score submissions on held-out data. Editions are versioned by year, and the label ontology has drifted over time — which matters a great deal here (a BraTS-2020-era label remap dragged onto 2024 data is exactly defect **D7**, §20). The **2024** edition is significant because it introduced the *post-treatment* glioma task (BraTS-GLI) — the first BraTS to ask models to segment brains *after* surgery and therapy rather than at diagnosis. Choosing the 2024 vintage is therefore the whole point of the project: it is what makes the resection-cavity class (label 4) exist at all.

**Evidence & Verify.** The dataset string in both `reports/splits.json` and `reports/data_provenance.json` names the 2024 BraTS-GLI mirror. For anything *absolute* about the challenge — its official case counts, geometry, or leaderboard scoring — I do not trust the community mirror; `reports/data_provenance.json` carries the citation to the official BraTS 2024 paper (`cite: "BraTS 2024 (de Verdier et al., arXiv:2405.18368) — PARTIAL community mirror"`), and flags itself as a partial mirror.

!!! gotcha "Watch out — mirror geometry"
    The mirror's volumes are **182×218×182**, a resampled, MNI-like grid — **not** the native BraTS geometry of **240×240×155**. Someone quoting the mirror as if it were the canonical release would be wrong. This is recorded verbatim in `reports/data_provenance.json` (`"geometry": "182x218x182 … native BraTS is 240x240x155 — provenance caveat"`).

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Is a community Kaggle mirror trustworthy enough to build on?"**
    For an internal, self-consistent A/B — yes, with caveats I state openly. The mirror is a *resampled* copy (182×218×182 MNI-like, not the native 240×240×155), so it is fine for comparing two pipelines against each other but I would not benchmark it against the official leaderboard. For any absolute claim about BraTS-2024, I cite the official paper (de Verdier et al., arXiv:2405.18368), not the mirror. The mirror's identity and its exact resampling caveat are pinned in `reports/data_provenance.json`, including a content hash so the specific bytes I used are reproducible.

### "post-treatment glioma"

**Claim.** *"post-treatment glioma."* These are gliomas imaged *after* clinical intervention — surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy — not at initial diagnosis.

**What it means.** A glioma is a tumour arising from the brain's glial (support) cells (the biology is derived from scratch in the explainer, §1). "Post-treatment" changes the segmentation problem fundamentally. After a neurosurgeon resects a tumour, the removed tissue leaves a **resection cavity (RC)** — a fluid- or blood-filled void with surgical margins — and the surrounding brain shows **treatment effects**: post-radiation gliosis, blood products, and enhancement that can mimic residual or recurrent tumour. Concretely, the post-treatment label set adds the RC class (label 4) on top of the pre-operative tumour sub-regions. This is *harder* than the pre-operative task for two reasons: (a) there are more, and more confusable, classes to separate — enhancing tumour vs. treatment-related enhancement look alike on a single sequence; and (b) the anatomy is distorted by the surgery itself, so the tidy tumour-plus-oedema geometry of a pre-op scan no longer holds.

The five labels in this ontology are: **0** background, **1** NETC (non-enhancing tumour core), **2** SNFH (surrounding non-enhancing FLAIR hyperintensity / oedema), **3** ET (enhancing tumour), **4** RC (resection cavity).

**Evidence.** How do I *know* the set is post-treatment and not a mislabelled pre-op batch? The label-presence audit answers it. Enhancing tumour (label **3**, ET) is present in **579 of 700** cases, and the resection cavity (label **4**, RC) in **580 of 700**. A pre-operative BraTS set uses the label vocabulary $\{0,1,2,4\}$ *without a genuine RC*; the co-presence of both a live ET class and an RC class across the corpus is the signature of the post-treatment 2024 vintage and rules out a mislabelled pre-op set.

**Verify.** These counts are the `label_present` block of `reports/dossier_facts.json` (`{"1": 353, "2": 700, "3": 579, "4": 580}` over 700 cases), regenerated by `scripts/compute_dossier_facts.py`.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"How do you actually know these are post-treatment scans and not a pre-op set?"**
    By auditing the labels rather than trusting the folder name. Enhancing tumour (label 3) appears in 579/700 cases and a resection cavity (label 4) in 580/700 — a pre-operative BraTS set would carry no genuine RC class. The presence of a real RC class across ~83% of the corpus is exactly the post-treatment signature, and it's recomputable from `reports/dossier_facts.json`. That audit is also what lets me assert the 5-class contract at load time and catch the reference's 4-class remap (D7).

### "MRIs (3 modalities)"

**Claim.** *"MRIs (3 modalities)."* Each case is a *multi-parametric* MRI: three co-registered sequences used as the three input channels — **t2f** (T2-FLAIR), **t1c** (post-contrast T1), and **t2w** (T2).

**What it means.** No single MRI sequence shows a whole glioma. MRI is *parametric*: by changing the acquisition you make different tissues bright, and each tumour sub-region is best seen on a different sequence (the physics is derived in the explainer, §1). Roughly: **t1c** (T1 after gadolinium contrast) lights up **enhancing tumour** where the blood–brain barrier is broken; **t2f** (FLAIR, which suppresses free fluid) reveals the **SNFH / oedema** halo; **t2w** delineates fluid and cavity boundaries. Feeding all three as channels lets the network exploit their complementary contrast — this is why the input has `in_channels = 3` (§3).

**Why three, not four?** The native T1 sequence (**t1n**) available in the release was deliberately **dropped**, matching the reference pipeline's channel selection. t1n carries the least sub-region-specific information once t1c is present (t1c is the same weighting *plus* contrast), so the marginal signal it adds is small relative to the extra I/O and memory per volume. Holding the channel set identical to the reference also keeps the A/B honest — the two tracks differ in *pipeline*, never in *inputs*.

**Evidence & Verify.** `reports/data_provenance.json` records `"modalities_kept": ["t2f", "t1c", "t2w"]` and `"label": "seg"`; the network's `in_channels = 3` is fixed in `configs/base.yaml` and reflected in the committed parameter count (§3).

!!! gotcha "Watch out"
    "3 modalities" is a *modelling* choice, not a limit of the data — the mirror also ships t1n. Don't imply the dataset only has three sequences; it has four and I use three, on purpose, to match the reference.

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Why throw away t1n — isn't more input always better?"**
    Not for free. t1n is native T1; t1c is the same weighting *after* contrast, so once you have t1c the extra sub-region-specific signal in t1n is marginal, while it costs an extra channel of I/O and memory per patch. More importantly, the reference pipeline used exactly {t2f, t1c, t2w}, and the whole project is a controlled A/B — matching its inputs byte-for-byte is what lets me attribute the Dice gap to the nine pipeline fixes rather than to a richer input. The kept-modality list is pinned in `reports/data_provenance.json`.

### "stratified" + "490/105/105 split"

**Claim.** *"a stratified 490/105/105 split."* The 700 cases are partitioned **70/15/15** into train (490), validation (105), and test (105), **stratified by resection-cavity presence**, with the partition **frozen at seed 42**.

**What it means.** A *split* separates the data by role so that the number you report is honest. **Stratified** sampling means the partition is drawn so that a chosen class-balance is preserved across all three subsets, rather than left to the luck of a random shuffle. Here the stratification variable is a per-case binary: *does this case contain a resection cavity (label 4)?* The RC class is the defining, and imbalanced, feature of the post-treatment task (present in ~83% of cases), so a naive random split could leave the small test set with an unrepresentative RC rate and make the test Dice a lottery. Stratifying on RC presence removes that particular source of split-luck.

The result is striking and is the single best evidence that the stratification actually fired: the resection cavity is present in **82.9% of cases in train, validation, AND test — identical to three significant figures.** Concretely the RC counts are 406/490, 87/105, and 87/105, each equal to $87/105 = 406/490 = 82.857\% \approx 82.9\%$.

![RC-present percentage per split (train/val/test), showing an identical 82.9% after stratifying on resection-cavity presence at seed 42.](reports/figures/dossier/stratification.png)

The three roles are not interchangeable — each subset is touched a different number of times, which is what keeps the final number honest:

| Split | n | Fraction | Role | How often it's touched |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| **train** | 490 | 70% | updates the network weights (back-propagation) | every optimiser step |
| **val** | 105 | 15% | **selects the checkpoint** (no gradient flows) | every epoch, read-only |
| **test** | 105 | 15% | the held-out estimate reported in the CV entry | **once**, after everything is frozen |

The seed matters as much as the ratio: fixing **seed 42** makes the exact 490/105/105 membership deterministic and identical across every run and both tracks. Track A and Track B therefore see the *same* 490 training cases and are judged on the *same* 105 test cases — without that guarantee the +0.321 voxel-wise Dice delta (§4) could be an artefact of a friendlier split rather than of the pipeline fixes.

**Verify.** The complete membership — seed, stratify key, and the per-split RC counts — is committed as `reports/splits.json` (`"seed": 42`, `"stratify": "rc_presence"`, `train {n:490, rc:406}`, `val {n:105, rc:87}`, `test {n:105, rc:87}`), and it lists every case ID by name so the partition is auditable case-by-case. The identical 82.857% figure and the figure above are regenerated by `scripts/compute_dossier_facts.py` → `reports/dossier_facts.json` (`stratification.splits`).

!!! example "Interview question"
    **"Why stratify, and why freeze the split?"**
    I stratify on resection-cavity presence because RC is the defining, imbalanced feature of the post-treatment task; a random 15% test set could easily land with an unrepresentative RC rate and turn the reported Dice into split-luck. Stratifying pins RC presence to an identical 82.9% in all three subsets (406/490, 87/105, 87/105), verifiable in `reports/splits.json`. I freeze the split at seed 42 so both tracks train on the same 490 cases and are scored on the same 105 — that's what licenses attributing the +0.321 delta to the nine pipeline fixes rather than to a luckier partition. Val selects the checkpoint; test is touched exactly once, at the very end.

!!! gotcha "Watch out — what the split does *not* buy"
    Stratifying by RC presence balances *one* binary. It does not balance the rarer classes (NETC is present in only 353/700 cases) or scanner/site distribution, and it says nothing about generalisation *beyond this mirror*. The honest uncertainty in the headline result is a single 105-case test set on one resampled mirror — a point made in full in §5, not hidden here.
